*Note: The compliance rate has dropped significantly (from ~50%) because the test coverage was expanded to include more directories (ML, Pandas, Errors, Logger) and the ignore list was updated with all failing tests in those areas.*

## Effort Scale
We use T-shirt sizes for relative effort estimation:
- **S**: Small (minor fixes, already done, or very simple tasks)
- **M**: Medium (some missing features, requires focused effort)
- **L**: Large (significant missing functionality, complex tasks)
- **XL**: Extra Large (massive gaps, requires major effort)

## AI Ease Scale
We estimate how easy it might be for AI-assisted development to quickly achieve compliance:
- **High**: AI can likely solve this with minimal guidance (e.g., pattern-based fixes, simple method implementations).
- **Medium**: AI can solve this if guided with specific patterns or examples (e.g., mapping options, standard SQL translations).
- **Low**: Requires deep architectural understanding, complex reasoning, or complex Calcite rules that AI typically struggles with without heavy human oversight.

- **Features**: Structured Streaming, source/sink connectors.
- **Categories**: `sql/streaming`, `sql` (Base) for operations.
- **Combined Effort**: **XL**
- **AI Feasibility**: Low.

### Style 3: Data Science & ML Prep
- **Features**: Pandas API on Spark, ML algorithms.
- **Categories**: `pandas` (Base), `ml`, `sql` (Base).
- **Combined Effort**: **XL** (While Pandas and ML might have Medium AI ease, they rely on Core SQL which is XL/Low).

*Conclusion*: The drop in compliance across all categories reinforces that Core `sql` is the primary blocker and requires manual, deep architectural work (Low AI Ease), while Pandas and ML gaps might be more approachable for AI assistance once the core is solid.
